# CarryOn™ — B2B Screening Brief
**For: Marketing team / Assistant intake script**
**Audience: B2B partnership leads (life insurance, financial planners, funeral homes, estate planners, employee-benefits brokers, faith communities, hospice, military/veteran orgs)**
**Purpose: Equip the founder's assistant to screen and qualify interested partners BEFORE they get on a discovery call with the founder. NOT a sales pitch script. The assistant's job is to listen, log, qualify, and book — not to demo or technically educate.**

---

## 0. Posture & guardrails for the assistant

You are NOT the salesperson. You are the **gatekeeper and qualifier**. Your job is to:

1. **Welcome warmly**, learn what brought them to us, capture the right details, and put them on the founder's calendar if they qualify.
2. **Confirm the platform is real and serious** — without demoing it, without going technical, and without making promises about specific features, integrations, timelines, or pricing.
3. **Hand off to the founder** for anything that touches: live demos, integration questions, white-label questions, pricing structure, contract terms, technical security details, data residency, or anything you're unsure of.

### Phrases you can lean on
- *"That's a great question for [Founder Name] — he handles every demo personally so the platform is shown the way he intends. Let me get you on his calendar this week."*
- *"I want to make sure he can answer that with the depth it deserves. Mind if I capture that and put it at the top of your discovery call?"*
- *"Everything CarryOn does is built around the family — what they need to know, find, and act on when something happens. He'll walk you through how that maps to your business specifically."*

### Phrases to AVOID
- ❌ "It works exactly like…" / "It integrates with X out of the box…" / "It costs $X per…"
- ❌ "Yes, we can build that" / "We can white-label" / "We have an API for that"
- ❌ Anything that sounds like a yes/no commitment about scope.

If a caller pushes for specifics, your line is: ***"He's the one who can give you the real answer on that — and he'll want to. Let me get you on his calendar."***

## 1. The platform in one breath

> **CarryOn™ is the digital family preparedness platform that brings together every aspect of a person's life — so they and their loved ones can carry on through anything.**

That's the whole pitch in one sentence. Don't elaborate beyond that on a screening call. The founder's demo is what closes; your job is to get them there.

If pressed for "what does it actually do":

> *"It's a single, secure platform where someone organizes their entire life picture — important documents, financial accounts, digital logins, who needs to be told what when something happens, recorded messages for loved ones at future life moments, and an AI guide that can answer their family's questions when they're not there to. It's built so the family is genuinely ready, not scrambling. [Founder] will walk you through the specifics."*

## 3. Use cases by partner vertical

For each vertical: **what they're solving for**, **which pillars resonate first**, and **the qualifying questions to ask**. Lead with their problem, not our features.

### A. Life Insurance Agents / Brokers

**What they care about:**
- Higher policy retention (clients lapse less when they feel "set up").
- Better claims experience (beneficiaries know the policy exists, can find it, can act on it without a 6-month battle).
- Differentiation in a commoditized market — they want to be the agent who *also* helped the family get organized.
- Compliance comfort: nothing in CarryOn replaces or alters the policy itself.

**Pillars that resonate first:** SDV (where the policy lives), EGA (so the family can ask "where's dad's policy"), FFN (the agent gets notified when a transition event occurs), IAC (claims-filing step lives in the checklist), CFP (policy shows up in the household financial picture).

**Qualifying questions:**
1. Are you looking for a tool to offer your existing book of business, or are you exploring this as a referral/affiliate channel for new client acquisition?
2. Roughly how many policies do you have under management?
3. Do you currently have any post-sale "family preparedness" or "legacy" service you offer clients today, even informally?
4. Are you part of a larger agency / IMO / FMO, or independent?

**Disqualify gently if:** they're really looking for a CRM, a quoting engine, or a lead-generation service. We're not those.

---

### B. Financial Planners / Wealth Advisors / RIAs

**What they care about:**
- Estate-planning gap: their clients have wealth but no organized "go-time" plan for the family.
- Practice differentiation: high-net-worth clients increasingly expect holistic family-readiness support.
- Continuity: when the primary client passes, the surviving spouse often leaves the advisor within 2 years. CarryOn keeps the family inside an organized hand-off.
- Fiduciary comfort: CarryOn doesn't give financial advice; it organizes what the advisor and client have already decided.

**Pillars that resonate first:** CFP (full household picture matches what the advisor already builds), SDV (estate documents in one place), EGA (family asks the AI, not the advisor at 11pm), MM (the human-legacy piece advisors can't deliver themselves), CCP (incapacity protocols), DAV (the digital-access gap most advisors quietly worry about).

**Qualifying questions:**
1. What does your current estate-organization handoff look like for a client family today?
2. Are you AUM-based, fee-only, hybrid? *(Just for context — affects how a partnership would feel for them.)*
3. Roughly how many client households, and what's the typical age range of the primary?
4. Do you work inside a broker-dealer / RIA umbrella, or independently?
5. Have you had a client family go through a transition event in the last 18 months? *(If yes, ask gently what that hand-off looked like — they'll usually tell you exactly what CarryOn solves.)*

---

### C. Funeral Homes / Cemetery Operators / Pre-Need Planners

**What they care about:**
- Pre-need conversion: families who plan ahead spend more, dispute less, and refer more.
- After-care: the bereaved family doesn't just need a service, they need *help with the next 90 days*.
- Differentiation from corporate consolidators (SCI, etc.) — independents need a digital story.
- Their families are often older and tech-anxious — they need something a 70-year-old will actually use.

**Pillars that resonate first:** IAC (this is the "first 30 days after death" surface), FFN (notifying the right people, in the right order, in the family's voice), MM (the legacy piece — funeral homes are increasingly asked for video tribute services), SDV (death certificate, obituary draft, service plan).

**Qualifying questions:**
1. Do you offer pre-need / pre-arrangement today, and what does that intake look like?
2. Are you independent, part of a regional group, or under a larger umbrella?
3. Do you have an after-care program — six-month follow-ups, grief resources?
4. Roughly how many services per year? *(Sizing question — DON'T quote pricing.)*
5. Are you exploring this as something you'd offer to families at intake, give as part of pre-need, or refer to as an aftercare partner?

---

### D. Estate Planning Attorneys / Trust & Estate Firms

**What they care about:**
- Their work product (the will, the trust, the POA) sits in a drawer until the day it's needed — and on that day, the family can't find it, doesn't understand it, and calls the attorney in a panic.
- Document delivery + family education is the bottleneck of their practice — they want the document used correctly, not just filed.
- Liability comfort: anything the family does inside CarryOn must not contradict or substitute for the legal instrument.
- They want to look modern to younger clients without learning new software themselves.

**Pillars that resonate first:** SDV (their documents live there, sealed and released correctly), EGA (the AI that explains the document to the family in plain English without giving legal advice), IAC (the action checklist their POA / executor will actually use), DAV (digital-asset access the will references but the family can never find), CCP (incapacity vs death protocols).

**Qualifying questions:**
1. How does your firm currently hand off the executed plan to the client family — copy, secure portal, document vault?
2. Do you offer plan-review or update services post-execution, or is it largely engagement-by-engagement?
3. Roughly how many active client families, and how many new plans per year?
4. Are you a solo / boutique firm, or part of a larger T&E practice?
5. Do you already use a document portal vendor (e.g. Trust & Will, Wealth.com, Vanilla, EncoreEstate)? *(Asking because that affects how a partnership would feel — NOT to compare features. If they ask 'how are you different,' say: "[Founder] handles that one — he'll walk you through it directly.")*

### E. Adjacent verticals (briefer)

- **Employee-benefits brokers / HR-tech** — selling CarryOn as a workplace benefit. Pillars: full nine, framed as financial-wellness + family-preparedness. Qualify on plan-sponsor count, age skew, current EAP/financial-wellness offering.
- **Hospice / palliative care providers** — the user has stated CarryOn is **free for every American in hospice care**, so this is a *referral / awareness* partnership, not a revenue partnership. Pillars: IAC, MM, SDV, FFN, CCP. Qualify on patient volume + service area.
- **Faith communities / clergy** — same family-preparedness frame, often paired with a "blessing the plan" intake. Pillars: MM (legacy messages), FFN (community notification), IAC. Qualify on congregation size + whether they're exploring it as a member benefit or a referral.
- **Military / veteran service organizations** — CarryOn has Military and Veteran tier discounts. Pillars: full nine, framed as "leave nothing for your family to figure out." Qualify on org type, member count, deployment cadence if active-duty.
- **Senior-living operators / CCRCs** — resident-onboarding and family-coordination angle. Pillars: full nine. Qualify on resident count, independent vs assisted vs memory-care mix.

## 4. Disqualifiers — when to politely NOT book the founder

You are doing the founder a favor by NOT booking these:

- ❌ **Vendors selling AT us.** "We have a CRM you should use", "We have a marketing service we'd love to discuss". Polite decline: *"Thank you — [Founder] handles vendor introductions through a separate channel. Feel free to email partnerships@carryon.us."*
- ❌ **Direct competitors fishing.** Estate-planning platform vendors, family-vault vendors, will-prep companies asking for a "partnership conversation" that's really a recon call. Politely take their info; let the founder decide.
- ❌ **Press / journalists.** Route to the founder via email, not a calendar call.
- ❌ **Consumers** who reach out thinking we're a B2B intake. Redirect them to **app.carryon.us** to sign up directly. *"This is our partner intake line — but you can sign up directly at app.carryon.us, and our hospice tier is free."*
- ❌ **Anyone asking for "the price sheet" before a discovery call.** Pricing is per-partnership; the founder structures it on the call. *"[Founder] does the pricing conversation himself because every partnership is shaped to the partner."*

## 5. Information to capture on EVERY screening call

Log these in the partnerships CRM / spreadsheet **before** booking the founder:

1. Full name, title, company, email, mobile.
2. Vertical (use the categories above).
3. Company size — rough headcount and rough client/policy/family count.
4. Independent vs. part of a larger entity.
5. **Why now?** What prompted them to reach out *this week*? (Single most predictive question.)
6. Are they a decision-maker, or scoping for one?
7. Any specific feature or use case they led with (note their words, verbatim).
8. Geography (state / region).
9. Anything they said they want the founder to know up front.
10. Source — how did they hear about us? (Paid, referral, press, conference, organic.)

## 6. Booking the founder — the close

Once they've cleared qualification, your line:

> *"Thank you — this is exactly the kind of conversation [Founder] takes personally. He runs every discovery and every demo himself, on his own demo account on the live platform. He's got [X] minutes [Tuesday/Thursday/etc.] — does either work? I'll send a calendar hold and a short prep note so he can come in already up to speed on what you shared with me."*

Send the calendar invite with:
- 30-minute hold (founder has stated this is video teleconference + live-platform demo).
- Zoom / Meet / whatever the founder uses.
- A pre-call note containing the 10 captured fields above.
- Subject line format: **"CarryOn × [Company] — [Vertical] discovery"**
