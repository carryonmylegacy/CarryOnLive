# CarryOn™ — Homepage Positioning & Narrative Strategy
*Prepared June 2026. Internal strategy document. Competitor named here for analysis ONLY — never appears on any public surface.*

## 0. Executive Summary

CarryOn is currently positioned as a **"family preparedness + estate planning platform."** That framing
sells the *storage* and the *estate* — it competes inside a category that already feels crowded and
slightly grim. The strategic move is to climb one level: from *"we organize what your family will need"*
to **"we keep your family functioning when a key person is suddenly unavailable."**

That higher category is **Family Continuity**. Estate planning is the *last* continuity event. Hospitalization,
deployment, travel, disaster, and incapacity are the others — and they happen far more often, to far more
people, while everyone is still alive to feel the relief. Owning continuity makes every narrower
solution (vaults, organizers, estate apps, "life planning" guides) feel like a single chapter of the book
CarryOn wrote.

**North Star for every word on the page:** the visitor leaves believing
> *"If something happens tomorrow, my family knows exactly what to do."*
Not *"my documents would be organized."*

| Dimension | What PharosLife does |

| **Transformation promised** | Scattered + overwhelmed → organized + guided + confident. |
| **Persona framing (their strongest asset)** | Three life-stages: *The One Holding It Together* (caregiver), *The One Left Figuring It Out* (survivor), *The One Planning Ahead* (planner). This is excellent and CarryOn must match its emotional range. |

### What PharosLife gets right (and CarryOn must absorb)
1. **Guidance over storage.** They never lead with "vault" or "documents." They lead with *"know what to do."* CarryOn's biggest weakness today is leading with Vault/Financial/Documents nouns.
2. **Calm, warm, non-legal tone.** No fear marketing, no survivalist edge, no insurance jargon.
3. **Three life-stage personas** that meet people *before, during, and after* — not only "when you die."
4. **Low activation pressure:** "You don't need to be ready to start. You just need to begin."

### Where PharosLife is structurally narrow (CarryOn's opening)
- It is **individual-centric**: *you* organize, *you* get guided. The family is the beneficiary of your work, not an active participant. There is no real *coordination layer* — no shared family channel, no per-person permissions, no succession chain, no delegated/trustee acting-on-behalf.
- It is **passive intelligence**: it "surfaces next steps based on your information," but there's no evidence of an AI that *reads inside your actual documents* to find gaps, contradictions, and deadlines, calibrated to your state's law.
- It addresses **one disruption shape** (a life change / a loss) — not the full spectrum (hospital stay, deployment, hurricane, incapacity, aging-parent support) *and* the final transition, each with its own playbook.
- It largely **stops at the planner and the survivor**. It does not carry the experience *through* transition with a concierge for the people who inherit.
- It is **pre-launch** and unproven; its trust rests on a founder promise, not on shipped security architecture.

### The larger problem that sits *above* PharosLife
> PharosLife answers: **"Is my information organized, and do I know what to do next?"**
> The bigger, unmet problem is: **"Will my *family* keep functioning — making decisions, paying what's due, reaching the right people, and staying connected — when I'm suddenly not the one holding it together?"**

That is not a *life-planning* problem. It is a **family-continuity** problem. It is multi-person, multi-event,
and spans before/during/after. **CarryOn must occupy that level.** Every outcome PharosLife promises
(organize, guide, assign, reduce overwhelm, peace of mind) becomes a *subset* of continuity — satisfied
in passing, then surpassed.

## 2. CarryOn Category Strategy

**Stop competing as:** an estate-planning app · a preparedness app · a secure vault · a document organizer ·
a "life planning dashboard."

**Start defining:** the **Family Continuity Platform** — the system a household runs *on* so it never stops
running, no matter who becomes unavailable or what disrupts it.

Three pillars of the category claim:
1. **Continuity, not storage.** The unit of value is *the family staying functional*, not files at rest.
2. **Every disruption, not only death.** Hospital stay, deployment, travel, disaster, incapacity, aging-parent
   care — *and* the final transition. One system, relevant **before, during, and after** each.
3. **The whole family, not one planner.** Coordination, permissions, succession, delegation, a shared channel,
   and an AI concierge for heirs — the family acts as a unit, not an audience.

Why this category is bigger (the math of relevance): a person plans their estate ~once. A family faces a
hospitalization, a deployment, a relocation, a storm evacuation, or an aging-parent crisis **many times across
a lifetime** — and CarryOn is the system they reach for *every* time, while everyone is alive to feel it work.
Estate transition becomes the final, fully-rehearsed instance of something the family already trusts.

## 4. Homepage Messaging Architecture

The page is built as a single argument that escalates: **disruption is normal → information isn't the
answer, knowing what to do is → here's the continuity timeline (before/during/after) → it works for every
disruption → it answers the questions that actually keep you up → here's how you build it → here's the
capability set as outcomes → trust → access → act.**

**Message spine (top to bottom):**
1. **Promise:** If something happens tomorrow, your family knows exactly what to do.
2. **Reframe:** This was never about finding files. It's about knowing what to do next.
3. **Category frame:** Continuity isn't a moment — it's *before, during, and after.*
4. **Breadth:** One system for every disruption, not just the final one.
5. **Resonance:** The questions every family is quietly afraid of — answered.
6. **Mechanism:** Build it calmly, at your pace, in five steps.
7. **Proof of depth:** Capabilities, told as continuity outcomes (pillars subordinated).
8. **Fit:** Built for how modern families actually live.
9. **Trust:** Bank-grade, human-verified, your-data-stays-yours.
10. **Mission/Access:** Hospice free, military/veteran, new adult.
11. **Act:** Start today.

**Tone rules:** calm, capable, protective, premium, reassuring, modern. Never fearful, never legalistic,
never insurance-y, never survivalist. Warmth + competence. Short sentences. Outcome verbs (*know, reach,
act, decide, keep going*) — not infrastructure nouns (*store, file, archive*).
